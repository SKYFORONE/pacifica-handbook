**What is Linear Regression Curve?**

Linear Regression Curve is an indicator that builds a “linear model of price behavior”. Simply put, it draws a smooth curved line that shows in which direction the price moves on average over a selected period.

This tool is especially useful for those who want to assess the overall direction of the market without too much noise, as well as to find deviations from normal movement.

**How does the indicator work?**

The indicator analyzes past prices and builds a linear regression curve based on them, which tries to describe the average price behavior as accurately as possible.

> Unlike a regular linear regression (straight line), smoothing is applied here to make the curve look more realistic and follow the market.

The longer the period, the smoother the line and the less it reacts to sharp fluctuations.

### How to read Linear Regression Curve?

If the curve is upward sloping, the market is in an uptrend.

![Image 3](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HLarIDOWsAAGcXL.jpg?name=large)

If the curve is downward sloping, the market is in a downtrend.

![Image 4](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HLar42yXQAAxlCS.jpg?name=large)

If the curve is horizontal, the market is flat.

It is also important to look where the price is relative to the curve:

Price above the curve, the market may be overbought.

Price below the curve,the market may be oversold.

✅ Advantages and ❌ Disadvantages

✅ Advantages:

Removes market noise.

Shows direction of movement clearly.

Helps to find overbought and oversold zones.

❌ Disadvantages:

May lag on sharp reversals.

Does not show volume or strength of movement.

Does not give specific entry points without additional tools.

Linear Regression Curve is a great visual tool for assessing the overall direction of the market. It is easy to use, makes the chart clearer and helps the trader to navigate the current situation. But like any indicator, it works best in conjunction with other indicators: RSI, volumes, candlestick patterns.

> adjust the period of the curve to your style, short periods are suitable for scalping, longer periods for swinging. And always remember: the trend is your friend, and the curve is your navigator!
