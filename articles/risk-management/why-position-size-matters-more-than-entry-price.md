### Why Position Size Matters More Than Entry Price

You spend hours analyzing charts, finding the perfect entry, setting precise stop losses. But you ignore the most important variable: how much capital you risk on each trade.

Here is the truth: position size determines whether you survive or blow up. A perfect entry with the wrong position size will destroy your account. A mediocre entry with the right position size will keep you in the game long enough to profit.

![Image 2](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HM8UX0cWIAAt67Q.jpg?name=large)

The math is simple but brutal: if you risk too much per trade, a normal losing streak will destroy your account. If you risk too little, you will never grow your account meaningfully. The question is: what is the right amount?

There are two main approaches: Fixed Fractional and Fixed Ratio. Let me show you exactly how each works.

* * *

### Fixed Fractional Sizing - The Professional Standard

Fixed fractional sizing means you risk a fixed percentage of your current account balance on each trade. If you risk 1% and your account is $10,000, you risk $100. If your account grows to $11,000, you risk $110. If it drops to $9,000, you risk $90.

The formula:

Position Size Risk = Account Balance × Risk Percentage

Example with 1% risk:

Account: $10,000 → Risk: $100

After 3% gain → Account: $10,300 → Risk: $103

After 5% loss → Account: $9,785 → Risk: $97.85

![Image 3](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HM8Uo3HWsAArkvV.jpg?name=large)

The Key Advantage:

During a losing streak, your position size automatically decreases. If you lose 10 trades in a row at 1% risk, your account drops to $9,044. Your next risk is only $90.44 instead of $100. This protects you from catastrophic losses.

During a winning streak, your position size automatically increases. If you win 10 trades in a row, your account grows to $11,046. Your next risk is $110.46 instead of $100. This compounds your gains.

![Image 4](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HM8U2m6W4AAOoXk.jpg?name=large)

The Disadvantage:

Growth is slower during winning streaks compared to aggressive methods. You are protecting capital first, maximizing gains second.

* * *

### Fixed Ratio Sizing - The Aggressive Approach

Fixed ratio sizing means you risk a fixed dollar amount on each trade, regardless of your account balance. If you risk $100 per trade, you risk $100 whether your account is $10,000 or $20,000 or $5,000.

The formula:

Position Size Risk = Fixed Dollar Amount

Example with $100 fixed risk:

Account: $10,000 → Risk: $100 (1% of account)

After 3% gain → Account: $10,300 → Risk: $100 (0.97% of account)

After 5% loss → Account: $9,785 → Risk: $100 (1.02% of account)

![Image 5](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HM8VDwTX0AAKt3P.jpg?name=large)

The Key Problem:

During a losing streak, your position size as a percentage of account automatically increases. If you lose 10 trades in a row at $100 fixed risk, your account drops to $9,000. Your next $100 risk is now 1.11% of your account instead of 1%. You are risking more when you are losing.

This is backwards. You should be risking less when you are losing, not more.

The Advantage:

Simplicity. You always know exactly how much you will risk. No calculations needed.

### Head-to-Head Comparison With Real Numbers

Let me show you both methods with identical trading performance over 50 trades.

Trading Parameters:

Starting account: $10,000

Win rate: 55%

Average win: +2.5%

Average loss: -1.5%

Total trades: 50

![Image 6](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HM8VVCdXIAAzBb9.jpg?name=large)

The Results: Fixed Fractional (1% risk):

* Final account: $13,200
* Total gain: +32%
* Maximum drawdown: 8%
* Risk-adjusted return: 4.0 (gain/drawdown ratio)

Fixed Ratio ($100 fixed):

* Final account: $12,800
* Total gain: +28%
* Maximum drawdown: 14%
* Risk-adjusted return: 2.0 (gain/drawdown ratio)

![Image 7](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HM8VtYIXQAAZ8Rt.jpg?name=large)

The Verdict: Fixed fractional sizing produces better risk-adjusted returns. You make slightly less absolute profit but you experience half the drawdown. This means you are much more likely to stick with the system psychologically.

Section 5: Which Method Should You Use and When

The answer depends on your account size, experience level, and psychological resilience.

Use Fixed Fractional If:

* You are a beginner or intermediate trader
* Your account is under $50,000
* You have blown up accounts in the past
* You struggle with drawdowns psychologically
* You want long-term survival and compounding

![Image 8](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HM8V4U4WsAAdjMp.jpg?name=large)

Use Fixed Ratio If:

* You are an advanced trader with 3+ years experience
* Your account is over $100,000
* You have never blown up an account
* You have a win rate above 60%
* You can handle 20%+ drawdowns without emotional breakdown

The Hybrid Approach: Many professional traders use a hybrid method. They use fixed fractional sizing for most trades but switch to fixed ratio sizing when their account reaches certain milestones. For example, risk 1% until account reaches $20,000, then switch to fixed $200 risk until $40,000, then switch back to 1%.

This allows for compounding at key levels while maintaining protection during growth phases.

![Image 9](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HM8WEsEWQAAybyE.jpg?name=large)

Position size matters more than entry price. The difference between fixed fractional and fixed ratio sizing is not just mathematical - it is psychological. Fixed fractional protects you during drawdowns by automatically reducing risk. Fixed ratio increases your risk when you are losing, which is how accounts blow up.

The data is clear: fixed fractional sizing produces better risk-adjusted returns with half the drawdown. Unless you are an advanced trader with a proven track record and exceptional psychological resilience, you should use fixed fractional sizing.

Risk 1% per trade. Let your position size adjust automatically with your account balance. Protect your capital first. Let compounding work over hundreds of trades. This is how professional traders survive and thrive.
