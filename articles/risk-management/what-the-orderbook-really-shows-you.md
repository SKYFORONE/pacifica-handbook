### What the Orderbook Really Shows You

The orderbook is a real-time list of all buy and sell orders waiting to be filled. It shows you the market's true supply and demand at every price level.

Bid side (left): Buy orders waiting to execute Ask side (right): Sell orders waiting to execute Spread: Difference between best bid and best ask

![Image 2](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HNBZQwiWYAEShvG.jpg?name=large)

**The orderbook tells you three critical things:**

1. Where liquidity is concentrated (support/resistance)
2. Whether buyers or sellers are more aggressive
3. Potential price manipulation (spoofing)

Most traders only look at price charts. Professionals watch the orderbook.

* * *

### Reading Liquidity Walls and Support/Resistance

A liquidity wall is a large order (or cluster of orders) at a specific price level. These walls act as support or resistance.

Buy Wall: Large bid order that supports price Sell Wall: Large ask order that resists price

**How to Identify Strong Walls:**

* Size: Wall should be 5-10x larger than average order size
* Persistence: Wall stays in place for multiple minutes/hours
* Historical significance: Price has reacted to this level before

**Weak Walls:**

* Small size (2-3x average)
* Disappear quickly when price approaches
* No historical significance

![Image 3](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HNBZuiUWAAAFuPN.png?name=large)

The Strategy: When you see a strong buy wall below current price, it provides support for long entries. When you see a strong sell wall above, it creates resistance for short entries.

But be careful: walls can be fake (spoofing). Let me show you how to identify them.

* * *

### Identifying Spoofing and Fake Liquidity

Spoofing is when traders place large orders to manipulate price, then cancel them before execution. They create fake liquidity walls to trick other traders.

**Signs of Spoofing:**

Sign 1: The Disappearing Wall A large wall appears, price moves toward it, then the wall vanishes instantly.

Sign 2: The One-Sided Wall Huge orders on one side but tiny orders on the other side at the same price level. This is often manipulation.

Sign 3: The Round Number Trap Massive walls at psychological round numbers ($70,000, $100, etc.) that disappear when tested. Spoofers know retail traders watch these levels.

* * *

How to Protect Yourself:

Rule 1: Wait for Confirmation Do not trade based on a wall alone. Wait for price to actually interact with the level and show real buying/selling pressure.

Rule 2: Watch Order Flow If a wall is real, you will see aggressive market orders hitting it and the wall holding firm. If it is fake, it will disappear or move away.

Rule 3: Check Multiple Timeframes Real liquidity walls appear across multiple timeframes. Fake walls only appear briefly on one timeframe.
