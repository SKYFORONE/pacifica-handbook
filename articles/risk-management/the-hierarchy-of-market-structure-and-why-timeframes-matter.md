### The Hierarchy of Market Structure and Why Timeframes Matter

Open any charting platform and you will see traders drawing horizontal lines everywhere. They mark every swing high, every swing low, every minor pause in price. Then they wonder why price seems to ignore half of them. The problem is not that support and resistance does not work. The problem is that they have no hierarchy.

Not all levels are created equal. A level formed on a weekly chart carries fundamentally different weight than a level formed on a 5-minute chart. The weekly level represents the collective decision of thousands of participants over hundreds of hours. The 5-minute level represents the noise of a few minutes. When these two levels conflict, the weekly level wins almost every time.

To trade with precision on Pacifica, you must build a multi-timeframe level hierarchy. This hierarchy has five distinct layers, each serving a specific purpose. The weekly timeframe defines the major structural battlefield. The daily and 4-hour timeframes build the intermediate grid. The 1-hour timeframe provides intraday context. The 15-minute and 5-minute timeframes deliver precision entry execution.

When you understand this hierarchy, you stop trading random lines. You start trading structural levels with mathematical significance. Let us break down each layer.

### Weekly Timeframe Mapping the Major Structural Levels

The weekly chart is your strategic map. It shows you where the big money has historically entered and exited the market. These levels are the ones that institutional algorithms watch, and they are the ones that produce the most explosive reactions when price returns to them.

To identify major weekly levels on Pacifica, apply the following quantitative algorithm.

**Rule One:**

Identify all swing points where price reversed by more than three times the 14-period Average True Range on the weekly chart. This filters out minor pauses and captures only significant structural turns. A reversal that does not exceed three ATRs is noise, not structure.

![Image 1](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMyVgIiXoAAlabQ.jpg?name=large)

**Rule Two:**

Count the number of touches. A level that has been tested three or more times on the weekly chart is a major structural level. Each touch represents a historical decision point where buyers or sellers stepped in with significant size. The more touches, the stronger the level, but also the weaker it becomes with each subsequent test because orders are being consumed.

![Image 2](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMyV3FNXMAATLam.jpg?name=large)

**Rule Three:**

I dentify the origin of major moves. Look for price levels where a multi-week trend began. These are the origin points of institutional positioning. When price returns to these levels, it often triggers a powerful reaction because they represent the average entry price of large players.

Rule Four: Mark the all-time high and all-time low of the asset on Pacifica. These are psychological and structural magnets. Price often respects these levels on the first test, then breaks through them with explosive momentum on subsequent tests as stop orders are triggered.

**Pacifica weekly chart showing the all-time high and all-time low marked with horizontal zones. Show price reaction at these levels**

Draw these levels as horizontal zones, not single lines. Use the weekly ATR to define the width of the zone. A level is not a price; it is an area where supply or demand has historically concentrated. On Pacifica, you can use the rectangle drawing tool to mark these zones clearly on your chart.

### Daily and 4-Hour Timeframes Building the Intermediate Grid

Once you have your weekly structural levels, you move down to the daily and 4-hour charts to build the intermediate grid. These levels are the ones you will trade most frequently. They are close enough to current price to be actionable, but significant enough to produce reliable reactions.

On the daily chart, apply the following rules.

Rule One: Identify swing points where price reversed by more than two times the 14-period ATR on the daily chart. These are intermediate structural levels.

![Image 3](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMyWyjOW8AA70Xx.jpg?name=large)

**Rule Two:**

Look for confluence with weekly levels. When a daily level aligns within half an ATR of a weekly level, the combined level becomes extremely powerful. This is called a confluence zone, and it is where the highest probability trades occur.

**Pacifica chart showing confluence. Display both weekly and daily levels that align. Mark the confluence zone clearly with a different color or thicker line**

![Image 4](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMyXivgXcAAW8Du.jpg?name=large)

**Rule Three:**

Mark the open, high, low, and close of significant daily candles, particularly the candles that formed at weekly levels. These levels act as intraday reference points.

**On the 4-hour chart, refine the grid further.**

![Image 5](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMyYYRjW4AA3Mhv.jpg?name=large)

**Rule One:**

Identify swing points where price reversed by more than 1.5 times the 14-period ATR on the 4-hour chart. These are local structural levels.

**Rule Two:**

Look for 4-hour levels that align with daily levels. These confluence zones are your primary trading areas.

**Rule Three:**

Identify fair value gaps and liquidity pools. On Pacifica, you can use the volume profile indicator to see where the most trading activity has occurred. These high-volume nodes act as powerful support and resistance levels.

![Image 6](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMyYtdAWgAAENNA.jpg?name=large)

The intermediate grid gives you a map of where price is likely to react on a daily to weekly basis. You do not trade every level. You wait for price to reach a confluence zone and then look for entry signals on lower timeframes.

### The 1-Hour Timeframe Context and Intraday Zones

![Image 7](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMyZLRnWIAAvY8n.jpg?name=large)

**The 1-hour chart** is your context layer. It does not provide entry signals, but it tells you the intraday bias and helps you refine your levels for the session.

**On the 1-hour char**t, apply the following rules.

Rule One: Identify the previous day high, previous day low, and previous day close. These are critical intraday reference levels. Price often reacts to these levels as algorithms and traders adjust their positions for the new session.

![Image 8](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMyZVqJXMAAakpX.jpg?name=large)

**Rule Two:**

Mark the Asian session high and low if you are trading the London or New York session. These levels often get swept before the real move begins.

**Rule Three:**

Identify 1-hour swing points where price reversed by more than one ATR. These are minor intraday levels that can act as short-term support and resistance.

**Rule Four:**

Use the 1-hour chart to confirm the direction of the intermediate trend. If the daily chart is bullish and the 1-hour chart is making higher highs and higher lows, you have strong intraday confirmation for long entries.

![Image 9](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMyZ66tXsAAaq1s.jpg?name=large)

The 1-hour chart is also where you refine your entry zones. When price approaches a daily or 4-hour confluence zone, drop to the 1-hour chart and look for a change in market structure. A break of the most recent 1-hour swing low in a bullish setup, or a break of the most recent 1-hour swing high in a bearish setup, confirms that the reaction is beginning.

### The 15-Minute and 5-Minute Timeframes Precision Entry Execution

This is where the money is made. The 15-minute and 5-minute charts are your execution layers. You do not analyze structure here. You execute entries based on the levels you have already identified on higher timeframes.

**On the 15-minute chart, apply the following entry algorithm.**

**Step One:**

Wait for price to reach a daily, 4-hour, or 1-hour confluence zone that you have pre-marked. Do not chase price. Let it come to your level.

**Step Two:**

Look for a liquidity sweep. Price often pierces the level by a few pips to trigger stop orders before reversing. On Pacifica, you can see this as a wick that extends beyond the level but closes back inside the zone.

![Image 10](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMybNCAXcAAmbvB.jpg?name=large)

**Step Three:**

Wait for a change in market structure on the 15-minute chart. In a bullish setup, this means price breaks above the most recent 15-minute swing high after tapping the level. In a bearish setup, price breaks below the most recent 15-minute swing low.

**Step Four:**

Enter on the retest of the broken structure or on the close of the breakout candle. Place your stop loss just beyond the liquidity sweep wick. This gives you a tight, logical stop that respects the structure of the trade.

![Image 11](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMycj3OXEAAPBaF.jpg?name=large)

Step Five: Target the next intermediate level on your grid. Your reward should be at least two times your risk. If it is not, skip the trade.

Building a multi-timeframe level hierarchy is the foundation of systematic trading on Pacifica. The weekly chart gives you the major structural battlefield. The daily and 4-hour charts build the intermediate grid where you find your trading zones. The 1-hour chart provides intraday context and confirmation. The 15-minute and 5-minute charts deliver precision entry execution.

Stop drawing random lines on your charts. Start building a hierarchy of levels with quantitative significance. Mark the zones where price has historically reacted with force. Wait for price to reach those zones. Execute with precision when the lower timeframe structure confirms the reaction.

This is not a secret strategy. It is a systematic process. And on Pacifica, with the right tools and the right discipline, it is a process that can transform your trading from gambling into a quantitative edge.
