What Is Spot Trading, Really?

Before we dive into Pacifica, let's make sure we're on the same page about what spot trading actually is.

**Spot trading** means buying or selling an asset for immediate delivery. You pay USDC, you get BTC. The transaction settles "on the spot" (hence the name).

**This is different from:**

* **Futures/Perpetuals**: You're trading contracts that derive value from the underlying asset. You don't actually own the BTC; you own a contract that tracks BTC's price.
* **Options**: You're buying the right (but not the obligation) to buy/sell at a specific price in the future.
* **Leveraged trading**: You're borrowing funds to amplify your position.

When you buy spot BTC, you own actual BTC. You can hold it, transfer it, use it as collateral, or just watch it go up (or down) over time.

Simple, right? So why does it matter where you do it?

### The Problem with Spot Trading on CEXs

Here's what I didn't realize for years: **centralized exchanges treat spot traders like second-class citizens.**

Think about it:

* CEXs make most of their money from derivatives trading (futures, options, perpetuals)
* Spot trading is just a gateway to get you to deposit funds
* The fees, the UI, the features—all optimized for active traders, not long-term holders

**The issues I ran into:**

**1. You Don't Really Own Your Crypto**

When you buy spot BTC on a CEX, you don't have the private keys. The exchange holds the BTC in their hot wallets. You have an IOU that says "this user owns X BTC."

We all know what happens when exchanges get in trouble. Your "spot BTC" becomes worthless if the exchange goes down.

**2. Withdrawal Friction**

Want to move your spot BTC to a hardware wallet? You pay withdrawal fees. You wait for processing. You deal with KYC checks.

This friction is designed to keep your funds on the exchange. The longer your money sits there, the more the exchange can use it for their own purposes (lending, market-making, etc.).

**3. Limited Utility**

Your spot BTC just sits there. On most CEXs, you can't use it as collateral for other trades. You can't earn yield on it (unless you use their "earn" products, which are just lending products with confusing names).

**4. Opaque Pricing**

CEXs often have slightly different prices than the broader market. They might show BTC at one price, but when you place a market order, you get filled at a slightly worse price due to spread and slippage.

For small trades, this doesn't matter. For large spot purchases, it adds up.

### Spot vs Perpetuals on Pacifica: When to Use Each

One question I get asked a lot: **If Pacifica has both spot and perpetuals, when should I use each?**

Here's how I think about it:

**Use Spot When:**

* You want long-term exposure to an asset (months or years)
* You believe in the fundamental value of the asset
* You want to earn potential future airdrops or governance rights
* You want to use the asset as collateral without liquidation risk
* You're building a core portfolio position

**Use Perpetuals When:**

* You want short-term exposure (hours, days, weeks)
* You want to use leverage to amplify returns
* You want to short an asset (bet against it)
* You want to hedge an existing position
* You're trading based on technical analysis or market sentiment

**The Pacifica Advantage:**

On most platforms, spot and perpetuals are completely separate. You have spot accounts and futures accounts, and moving money between them is a hassle.

This is incredibly powerful. It means I don't have to choose between being a long-term holder and an active trader. I can be both, simultaneously, with the same capital.

**Strategy 2: Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA) with Purpose**

Instead of just buying spot BTC every week and letting it sit, I:

* Buy spot SOL on Pacifica
* Let it count as collateral
* Open a small perpetual position (2-3x leverage) on a different asset
* Use the profits from the perpetual to buy more spot SOL

This creates a feedback loop where my spot holdings enable more trading, which generates profits to buy more spot.

**Strategy 3: Volatility Harvesting**

When the market is highly volatile:

* I hold spot BTC/ETH (long-term bullish)
* I open short perpetual positions on overextended altcoins
* If the market dumps, my short positions profit, and I use those profits to buy more spot at lower prices
* If the market pumps, my spot holdings appreciate, and I close the shorts at a loss (which is fine, it's hedging)

This strategy works beautifully with Unified Margin because my spot holdings provide the collateral for the shorts.

**Strategy 4: Funding Rate Arbitrage**

This is a more advanced strategy:

* I hold spot SOL (long-term)
* I open a SOL perpetual short (same size)
* Result: I'm market-neutral (price movements cancel out)
* I profit from funding rates (when perpetual traders pay to hold long positions)

On Pacifica, my spot SOL counts as collateral for the short position, so I don't need additional capital. This is a low-risk way to earn yield on my spot holdings.
