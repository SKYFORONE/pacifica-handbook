**Section 1: FOMO - The Fear of Missing Out That Makes You Buy Tops**

FOMO is the most expensive emotion in trading. You see a green candle pumping 15%. Your friend tweets about 300% gains. The chart looks like it is going straight up. You cannot take it anymore. You buy at the top. Then price reverses immediately.

This happens because FOMO bypasses your rational brain. You stop thinking about risk-reward. You stop checking your levels. You just click buy.

The Pattern:

1. Price pumps 20-30% in a short time
2. Social media explodes with gain posts
3. You feel anxiety watching from the sidelines
4. You convince yourself "this time is different"
5. You enter without a stop loss
6. Price reverses and you panic sell at a loss

The Solution: Create a FOMO checklist. Before any trade, answer these questions:

* Did I identify this level BEFORE the pump?
* Is my risk-reward at least 1:2?
* Do I have a stop loss in place?
* Am I entering because of analysis or because of emotion?

If you cannot check all four boxes, you are FOMOing. Walk away.

**Section 2: Revenge Trading - The Emotional Spiral That Blows Accounts**

You just lost 3% on a trade. You are angry. You feel like the market stole from you. You immediately open a new position, twice the size, to win back what you lost. You skip your analysis. You ignore your rules. You just want revenge.

This is how accounts go from -3% to -50% in one day.

The Psychology: Revenge trading is not about money. It is about ego. Your brain cannot accept being wrong. It wants to prove the market wrong. So you double down, triple down, until you blow up.

The Pattern:

1. You take a loss (even a small one)
2. You feel anger and frustration
3. You immediately look for the next trade
4. You increase position size to "make it back faster"
5. You skip your normal analysis
6. You take another loss
7. You repeat until you hit your daily loss limit or blow the account

The Solution: The Two-Loss Rule. If you lose two trades in a row, you MUST stop trading for 24 hours. No exceptions. Close the platform. Go for a walk. Do anything except trade.

Section 3: Overconfidence - The Silent Killer After Winning Streaks

You just won five trades in a row. Your account is up 12%. You feel like a genius. You start thinking you have figured out the market. You increase your position size from 1% to 5%. You remove your stop loss because "you know" the trade will work. Then one trade wipes out all your gains.

The Psychology: Overconfidence is more dangerous than FOMO or revenge trading because it feels good. You are winning. You feel smart. You do not realize you are taking excessive risk until it is too late.

The Pattern:

1. You have a winning streak (3-5 trades)
2. You attribute wins to skill, not variance
3. You increase position size
4. You relax your rules (skip stop losses, ignore levels)
5. You take lower quality setups
6. One bad trade wipes out multiple wins
7. You spiral into revenge trading

The Solution: The Winning Streak Protocol. After three consecutive wins, you MUST do the following:

* Reduce position size by 50% for the next trade
* Write down three reasons why your wins might be luck, not skill
* Review your last three trades and identify what could have gone wrong
* Wait at least 4 hours before taking the next trade

**Section 4: The Three-Step Framework to Eliminate Emotional Trading**

You cannot eliminate emotions. They are part of being human. But you can build systems that protect you from your emotions.

Step One: The Pre-Trade Checklist Before every single trade, you must complete this checklist:

* I identified this level before price reached it
* My risk is exactly 1% or less
* My reward is at least 2x my risk
* I have a stop loss in place
* I am not in a winning or losing streak (or I am following the protocol)
* I am trading the plan, not my emotions

If you cannot check all six boxes, you do not take the trade. Period.

* * *

Step Two: The Post-Trade Journal After every trade, you must record:

* Entry price, stop loss, take profit
* Position size and risk percentage
* Why you took the trade (specific level, indicator, setup)
* Your emotional state (calm, anxious, excited, angry)
* Whether you followed your rules (yes/no)
* What you learned

Review this journal every Sunday. Look for patterns. Are you losing more when you trade emotionally? Are you winning more when you follow your checklist?

* * *

Step Three: The Weekly Review Every Sunday, spend 30 minutes reviewing:

* Your win rate for the week
* Your average win vs average loss
* Your biggest mistake
* Your best trade
* Your emotional patterns
* Your rule-following percentage

Set one specific goal for next week. Not "make more money". Something like "follow my checklist 100% of the time" or "no revenge trades".

* * *

FOMO makes you buy tops. Revenge trading makes you spiral into losses. Overconfidence makes you ignore risk. These three emotions destroy more accounts than bad strategies ever will.

The solution is not willpower. Willpower fails when emotions run high. The solution is systems. Use the pre-trade checklist to block emotional entries. Use the post-trade journal to identify patterns. Use the weekly review to stay accountable.

Trading is 20% strategy and 80% psychology. Master your emotions, and you master trading.
