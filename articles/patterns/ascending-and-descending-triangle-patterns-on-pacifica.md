**What is this pattern?**

“Bearish Engulfing” (Bearish Engulfing) is a candlestick pattern that appears after a rise and warns:

👉 “Watch out, the bulls are deflating, the bears are coming in!”

It is a signal for a possible downward trend reversal.

_**In this article, I will explain in simple terms how the pattern works on Pacifica, how to use it correctly and what you should pay attention to**_**.**

What does a bearish takeover look like?

The first candle is an uptrending candle (green or white) and its body is small.

![Image 2](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMIwP7XWsAAxC9s.jpg?name=large)

The second candle is bearish (red or black) and its body completely covers the body of the first candle.

Both candles appear after the rise.

![Image 3](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMIwjetXcAAgG8V.png?name=large)

That is, the second candle absorbs the first candle completely hence the name.

![Image 4](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMIxX9jXgAAzaNu.jpg?name=large)

**What does it mean?**

Buyers tried to continue the growth (first candle).

But sellers took over and closed the day below the previous day’s opening.

This is a clear reversal signal especially if it appears at strong resistance or after a long rise.

**📊 Where does it work best**?

On daily and 4H charts.

After long green candles or several days of gains.

Near key levels: resistances, trend lines, overbought zones (by RSI, for example).

**✅ Example on the chart**

Price rises from 80 to 100.

A small green candle appears at the level of 100.

The next day a fat red candle that opens above and closes below the body of the previous candle.

This is a bearish takeover.

A pullback or reversal is possible. You can short with a stop above the high.

![Image 5](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMIyWLSXoAAQ_y0.png?name=large)

**How to trade?**

**1️⃣ Entry**

Enter short after closing of the second (engulfing) candle.

You can enter aggressively at the opening of the next candle.

Or cautiously after retesting the level (if the price rises a bit, but does not break the maximum).

![Image 6](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HMIy1ioW8AEM3NC.jpg?name=large)

**2️⃣ Stop Loss**

Set it just above the maximum of the engulfing candle.

**3️⃣ Take Profit**

The first target is the nearest support level.

The second target is a trailing stop or fixed R:R (for example, 1:2)..

* * *

**✅ Pros and ❌ Cons**

**✅ Advantages:**

Easy to visually recognize.

Works on all timeframes and instruments.

Blends well with other signals.

**❌ Disadvantages:**

May be false without confirmation.

Works worse in sideways markets.

Requires context not a panacea in itself.

**📌 Tips for usin**g

Confirm the signal with volumes the volume on the second candle should be higher.

Use levels, RSI or MACD to reinforce the signal.

Avoid trading the pattern near support it may not work.

A bearish engulf is an “exclamation mark” after a rise. It says:

“The bulls are tired. The bears are taking the lead.”

> It’s not a reason to panic, but a great chance for a short entry point especially if you see that the market is overheated and the zone above hasn’t been broken for a long time.
