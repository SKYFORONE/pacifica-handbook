What is it?
Triple top and triple bottom patterns are chart patterns that signal the end of the current trend and a possible reversal in the opposite direction.


Triple top is  appears after growth, indicates weakening of buyers and downward reversal.


Triple bottom is appears after a fall, indicates the exhaustion of sellers and a reversal upwards.


> *In this article, I will explain in simple terms how the pattern works on Pacifica, how to use it correctly and what you should pay attention to.*

## What does a “triple peak” look like?


Three peaks at about the same level.


Between them are two troughs (small pullbacks).


Below the troughs is a support line (called the neckline).


If the price breaks the neckline downwards, it is a short signal.


![Illustration 2](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HL0jcxkXYAA-VyU.jpg)


Example:


Peaks: 100 → 99.5 → 100.2


Troughs: 97.5 → 97


Breakdown of the level 97 — entry to sell.


Target: distance from the peak to the neck (3 points), target = 94…


![Illustration 3](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HL0jjg6XMAAp_zo.png)


What does a “triple bottom” look like?
Three troughs roughly on the same line.


Between them there are two pullbacks upwards.


There is a resistance line above the tops of these pullbacks.


Breaking it up → a buy signal.

**Example:**


Troughs: 45 → 44.8 → 45.1


Vertices in between: 47


Breakdown of the level 47 — entry into long.


Target: 47 + (47–45) = 49


## 💡 How to trade?


Wait for the neckline breakdown, don’t guess in advance!


Enter after confirmation (better, on retest).


Stop loss, behind the last peak or trough.


Take Profit, at the height of the figure.


> 🧠 Tip: These patterns work better if they are accompanied by:


Decreasing volumes at each top/trough.


Confirmation from indicators (RSI, MACD).
