## What Exactly is a Pacifica Vault?


According to the official Pacifica documentation, a vault is defined simply and clearly: "A vault is a managed trading pool deployed by a Pacifica user.


Instead of executing trades yourself, you delegate the execution. Depositors contribute USDC to the pool, and a designated "vault manager" trades that pooled balance on the Pacifica perpetual exchange


This creates a powerful symbiosis:

* Depositors get access to professional-grade trading strategies and yield generation without staring at charts.
* Vault Managers get access to deeper liquidity and can earn performance fees based on their trading success.

It is a diversified trading ecosystem designed to make sophisticated strategies accessible to everyone.

## How Depositing Works (The USDC Standard)


The process of entering a vault is designed for maximum simplicity and security.

1. USDC Only: Depositors contribute USDC to the vault. This provides a stable, predictable baseline for measuring the manager's performance, free from the volatility of the collateral asset itself.
1. Seamless Integration: Because Pacifica uses a unified spot-and-collateral margining system, your deposited USDC is efficiently allocated to the vault manager’s trading account.
1. Deposit Caps: Vault managers can set a "vault deposit cap" to ensure the strategy remains effective and does not become too large to execute properly.

> *Action Step: 
Always check if a vault has reached its deposit cap before attempting to fund it. If it is full, you may need to join a waitlist or find an alternative vault with a similar strategy.*

## The 5 Built-In Risk Controls You Must Know


Pacifica does not leave your capital to chance. The documentation explicitly states that "a vault is a real Pacifica account and is subject to the same liquidation engine as any other account.


On top of this foundational security, vaults are equipped with specific, hard-coded risk controls:

1. Symbol Whitelists: The manager can only trade specific, pre-approved perpetual pairs. They cannot suddenly open a highly leveraged position in an obscure, volatile altcoin.
1. Leverage Caps: A maximum leverage limit is enforced at the vault level, preventing the manager from taking on catastrophic, account-blowing risk.
1. Manager Balance Requirements: The vault manager must have their own skin in the game, ensuring their interests are aligned with the depositors.
1. Trading Halts: In extreme market conditions, trading can be paused to protect the pool from irrational volatility.
1. Vault Liquidation: If the vault's equity falls below the maintenance margin, the standard Pacifica liquidation engine will step in to close positions, preventing negative balance and protecting the protocol's solvency.

## Understanding Vault Profit & Loss


How do you make money? The vault manager trades the pooled USDC balance on the perpetual markets.Gross Profit: Generated from successful directional trades, funding rate arbitrage, or market-making strategies executed by the manager.

* Gross Profit: Generated from successful directional trades, funding rate arbitrage, or market-making strategies executed by the manager.
* Fees: The manager typically takes a performance fee (a percentage of the profits) and sometimes a small management fee. This is clearly displayed in the vault's specifications.
* Net PnL: Your share of the vault's net profit, proportional to your USDC contribution.

The Pacifica API and UI provide transparent "Profit & Loss" tracking, allowing you to see the vault's historical performance and your exact share balance at any time.

## Is a Vault Right for Your Portfolio?


Vaults are a powerful tool, but they are not for every dollar you own. Use this quick checklist:


✅ Use Vaults If: 
You have idle USDC, believe in a specific manager’s track record, and want passive exposure to perpetual markets without active management.


❌ Do Not Use Vaults If: 
You need instant access to your funds (withdrawals are processed by the vault's logic, not instantly), or you want to manually control every entry and exit.
