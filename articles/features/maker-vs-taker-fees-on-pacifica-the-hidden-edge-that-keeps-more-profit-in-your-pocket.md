## The Reality of Trading Fees


When you calculate your potential profit on a trade, it is easy to focus only on the price movement. But there is a silent friction that eats into every single trade: fees.


If you are scalping or day trading, taking 10-20 trades a week, fees can easily consume 10% to 20% of your gross profits if you are not careful. The good news? On a professional platform like Pacifica, you have complete control over how much you pay. You just need to understand the difference between providing liquidity and taking it.


---


## Maker vs. Taker: The Core Difference


Every order book relies on two types of participants. Pacifica, like all top-tier exchanges, incentivizes one and charges a premium for the other.


The Taker (Higher Fee)A Taker is someone who demands immediate execution. When you place a Market Order, you are "taking" existing liquidity off the order book. Because you are consuming liquidity and demanding instant service, you pay the higher Taker fee.


The Maker (Lower Fee)A Maker is someone who provides liquidity to the market. When you place a Limit Order at a specific price that does not immediately fill, your order rests on the order book. You are "making" the market deeper for everyone else. As a reward for providing this service, Pacifica charges you a significantly lower Maker fee.


---


## How to Guarantee the Lower "Maker" Fee


Knowing the difference is half the battle. Executing it consistently is the professional edge. Here is how to ensure you always pay the Maker fee.


Rule 1: Default to Limit Orders
Make it a habit to use Limit orders for your entries and your Take Profits. Patience is literally rewarded with lower fees.


Rule 2: Use the "Post-Only" Feature
This is the most powerful tool in your arsenal. When you check the "Post-Only" box on a Limit order, you are giving the Pacifica engine a strict instruction: "Only place this order if it will rest on the book as a Maker. If the current market price would cause it to fill immediately as a Taker, cancel it instead."


This completely eliminates the risk of accidentally paying a Taker fee during a fast-moving market.

## The Math: How Fees Compound Over Time


Let’s look at why this matters with a realistic example.


Imagine you execute $100,000 in total trading volume over a month (e.g., ten trades of $10,000 each).

* If you use Market orders (Taker) at a hypothetical 0.05% fee, you pay $50 in fees.
* If you use Limit orders (Maker) at a hypothetical 0.02% fee, you pay $20 in fees.

That is a $30 savings on just $100k volume. For an active trader doing $1,000,000 in volume, that is a $300 difference. Over a year, optimizing your fee tier can literally pay for your trading software, your data feeds, or a nice vacation. It is free money left on the table if you ignore it.
