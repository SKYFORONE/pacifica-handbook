What is Ratio?
Ratio is an indicator used to analyze the relationship between prices, volumes, or other market indicators. Depending on what data is taken into account, it can show the strength of a trend, market sentiment, or the likelihood of a reversal.


👉 Main idea:
Ratio helps to compare two values ​​to understand which asset or direction of movement is stronger.


> *In this article, I will explain in simple terms how the indicator works on Pacifica, how to use it correctly and what you should pay attention to.*


![Illustration 1](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HKnwVjIWIAArxsG.jpg)

What are the Ratios?
🔹 1. Price Ratio
Used to compare the prices of two assets.


📌 Example:


If the Ratio is growing, it means that asset A is becoming stronger relative to asset B.


**✅ Usage:**


Comparing stocks in the same industry.


Assessing the strength of currency pairs (e.g. BTC/USDC vs ARB/USDC).


Analysis of gold and the dollar (Gold/USD Ratio).​


Volume Ratio


Compares the volumes of purchases and sales to determine who controls the market — bulls or bears.


📌 Example:


If Ratio > 1, then the market is under buyers’ control.


If Ratio < 1, then sellers dominate.


**✅ Usage:**


Determining pressure from large players.


Filtering false breakouts.


3. Put/Call Ratio (option indicator)
Shows the ratio of PUT options (for sale) to CALL options (for purchase).


**📌 Example:**


If Ratio > 1, it means that traders are betting more on a fall.


If Ratio < 1, purchases prevail.


✅ Usage:


Determining panic in the market (if Ratio is growing sharply).


Filtering signals to buy or sell.

## How to use Ratio in trading on Pacifica?


📌 1. Determining the strength of the trend


If the Price Ratio is growing → asset A is stronger than asset B, we can consider buying.


![Illustration 3](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HKnx3VoWcAARb_f.jpg)


If the Ratio is falling → asset B is dominant, we look for selling.


![Illustration 4](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HKnx8OvXkAAHTRE.jpg)


2. Market sentiment analysis


High Put/Call Ratio (>1.2) → market participants expect a fall (it may be worth buying against the crowd).


![Illustration 5](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HKnyAekXQAA0DJf.jpg)


Low Ratio (<0.7) → traders are too optimistic, a downward reversal is possible.


📌 3. Filtering false signals


If the price breaks through the level, but the Volume Ratio is low, then the movement is weak, and the breakout may be false.


![Illustration 6](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HKnyEXsWwAATdLi.jpg)


---


If the Volume Ratio is high, then the movement is confirmed by volumes.


Ratio Trading Examples


🔹 Scenario 1: Stock Strength Analysis
We compare Tesla (TSLA) and S&P 500 (SPX).


If Ratio (TSLA/SPX) is rising, then Tesla is growing faster than the market.
✅ Solution: Consider going long in Tesla.


🔹 Scenario 2: Looking for panic with Put/Call Ratio
Put/Call Ratio has sharply increased above 1.5.


This means that the market is expecting a fall, but too many traders are already short.


✅ Solution: A reversal upwards is possible, we are looking for longs.


🔹 Scenario 3: Filtering breakouts with Volume Ratio
The price has broken through resistance, but Volume Ratio < 1.


This means that volumes do not support the movement.
❌ Solution: We do not enter, we are waiting for confirmation.


Ratio is a powerful tool that helps you evaluate the strength of a trend, volumes, and market sentiment.


✔ Asset comparison helps you find the strongest instruments to trade.
✔ Volume Ratio shows whether the movement is confirmed by volumes.
✔ Put/Call Ratio is useful for analyzing crowd sentiment.


> Use Ratio along with RSI, MACD, and support/resistance levels for more accurate signals!
