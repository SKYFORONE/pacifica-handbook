## The Core Difference: Speed vs. Price


Every time you click "Buy" or "Sell" on Pacifica, you are making a fundamental choice: do you value speed more, or do you value price more?


This is the difference between Market and Limit orders.


Market Order: "Get me in NOW, at whatever the current price is." You are demanding immediate execution. Limit Order: "Get me in ONLY at this specific price (or better)." You are demanding price precision, and you are willing to wait.


Understanding this simple dichotomy is the first step to professional trade execution.


---


## The Hidden Cost of Market Orders


Market orders are tempting. The market is pumping, you feel FOMO, you click "Market Buy", and you are in. But this convenience comes with two hidden costs.


Cost 1: Taker Fees Because you are "taking" liquidity off the orderbook, you pay the Taker fee, which is higher than the Maker fee on Pacifica. Over hundreds of trades, this fee difference compounds significantly.


Cost 2: Slippage In fast-moving markets, the price you see on the screen is not the price you get. By the time your market order hits the matching engine, the best available price might have moved. You end up buying higher (or selling lower) than you intended.


**When to use Market Orders:**

* During extreme volatility when a breakout is confirmed and you MUST get in immediately.
* When closing a losing position urgently to prevent further damage.
* When trading highly liquid pairs (like BTC-PERP or SOL-PERP) where slippage is minimal.

---


## The Power of Limit Orders


Limit orders are the tool of patient, professional traders. You set your price, and the Pacifica engine waits for the market to come to you.


The Benefits:

* Zero Slippage: You will never pay more (or receive less) than your specified price.
* Maker Fees: By placing a limit order that rests on the orderbook, you "make" liquidity. Pacifica rewards this with lower Maker fees.
* Psychological Discipline: It forces you to plan your entry in advance, reducing emotional, impulsive trading.

When to use Limit Orders:

* When entering a trade at a specific support or resistance level.
* When taking profit (always use limit orders to secure your gains at your target).
* In low-liquidity markets where market orders would cause massive slippage.

## Pro Tools: Stop-Limit and Post-Only


Once you master the basics, Pacifica offers advanced order types to refine your execution.


Stop-Limit
This is a two-part order. First, a "Stop" price triggers the order. Second, a "Limit" price is placed on the orderbook. It is perfect for breakouts: you tell Pacifica, "If price hits $70,000, place a limit order to buy at $70,050." It prevents you from chasing a fakeout with a market order.


Post-Only
This is a hidden gem. When you check "Post-Only", you guarantee that your limit order will only be added to the orderbook as a Maker order. If the market price is already at your limit price, the order will automatically cancel rather than executing as a Taker market order. This guarantees you always pay the lower Maker fee.
